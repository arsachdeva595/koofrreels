🎬 Reels — your own footage

Turn the video clips you already have stored in Koofr cloud into a polished vertical reel, with AI-chosen clips, title/caption overlays, and an optional voiceover.

When to use this

Use Reels when you have your own real footage — product shots, lifestyle clips, behind-the-scenes — already uploaded to Koofr. This is the most authentic mode: nothing is AI-generated or stock; it stitches and polishes your clips.

Needs: Koofr (email + app password) · Anthropic API key (for AI clip picking & text) · ElevenLabs key (optional, only for voiceover). Set these in Settings → API Keys.

Choosing where clips come from

SourceWhat it does
✦ AI picksClaude scores every clip in your Koofr library by its filename and picks the ones that best match your prompt. Easiest option.
🎬 BrowseType a Koofr folder path, click Load, and hand-pick the exact clips you want.
📁 FolderUse all clips inside a folder path you specify.
🎲 RandomPicks clips at random from your library — great for quick tests.

How to make a Reel

  1. Open the 🎬 Reels tab.
  2. In "What's your reel about?" describe the vibe/theme (e.g. "golden hour at the beach, summer energy").
  3. Under "Where to pull clips from" pick a source pill (AI picks / Browse / Folder / Random) and, for Browse/Folder, enter the Koofr path.
  4. Set Duration (15–90s) and a Music track (or leave "Random from library").
  5. Toggle Add title & caption overlay and Generate voiceover as desired.
  6. Click ✦ Generate Reel. When the storyboard is ready it pauses for your review — see Review & Approve.
  7. After rendering, preview, then download or publish.
💡 Name your Koofr clips descriptively (e.g. beach-sunset-slowmo.mp4). "AI picks" reads filenames, so good names = better matches.

🎞 Stock — Pexels stock video

No footage of your own? Claude writes a full scene-by-scene storyboard, then finds a matching Pexels stock video for every scene and assembles them into a reel.

When to use this

Use Stock for concept, educational, or marketing reels where you don't have your own clips — e.g. motivational, tips, or storytelling content that just needs good-looking generic b-roll behind the message.

Needs: Pexels API key (free) · Anthropic API key · ElevenLabs key (optional, voiceover). Set in Settings → API Keys.

How to make a Stock Reel

  1. Open the 🎞 Stock tab.
  2. Write your topic/message in the prompt (e.g. "the cost of not starting your business today").
  3. Set Duration and Music.
  4. Toggles: title & caption overlay, voiceover, and Use brand guidelines (applies your Brand Voice from Settings).
  5. Advanced: Skip storyboard — use my own script lets you paste a scene-by-scene script directly instead of having Claude write one.
  6. Click 🎞 Generate Stock Reel, then review & approve the storyboard.
  7. Preview → download or publish.
ℹ️ Each scene's stock clip is found using the AI-generated "clip hint" from the storyboard. If a scene looks off, you can re-generate or tweak your prompt to steer the visuals.

🤖 AI Reels — 100% generated footage

Every clip is generated from scratch — fully original footage that exists nowhere else. The most powerful and brand-controllable mode. You choose which video model generates the clips (Google Veo, fal.ai / Kling, or WaveSpeed) in Models & Cost.

When to use this

Use AI Reels when you want completely original, on-brand visuals with no stock footage and no need to film anything — including showcasing a product, a recurring character, or pure scenery.

Needs: Anthropic API key (script) · a configured video model (Google Veo / fal-Kling / WaveSpeed — see Models & Cost) · an image provider for the keyframe stills (fal / Vertex / WaveSpeed — same setting as Cinematic) · ElevenLabs key (optional, voiceover). If no image provider is set it falls back to generating video directly (no keyframe review).
🆕 Every AI reel type now works keyframe-first, just like Cinematic: it generates a still per scene → you review/reshoot them → then makes the video from the approved stills. You also see an estimated cost when you approve the script, and you approve the spend before anything is generated.

Script framework

The selector at the top of AI mode controls the storytelling structure Claude uses:

FrameworkBest for
ABTAnd · But · Therefore — classic story tension (default).
PASProblem · Agitate · Solve — pain-point selling.

How to make an AI Reel

  1. Open the 🤖 AI Reels tab and pick a Script framework and a Reel type. Upload the reference image(s) the type needs (creator, product, or both).
  2. Write your prompt, set Duration and Music, and the toggles (voiceover, title/caption, brand guidelines).
  3. Click Generate. Gate ① — Script & Cost: review/edit the script and check the estimated cost. Approving approves the spend.
  4. Gate ② — Keyframe review: the app makes a still per scene; preview them and reshoot any you don't like (cheap) before video is generated.
  5. It then makes the video from your approved stills, times it to your voiceover, and composes the reel.
  6. Preview → download or publish (or grab it later from History).

ℹ️ Generated clips are a few seconds each (Veo up to 8s, Kling a 5s floor). Generation takes a few minutes. The keyframe review needs an image provider configured; without one it falls back to direct video (no still review). Pick models in Models & Cost.

🆕 Fixed: Veo landscape output

Google Veo used to default to 16:9 landscape even when the scene started from a 9:16 keyframe still — the app then blurred/padded it to fit vertical, which softened the shot. Veo now explicitly requests 9:16 vertical output, so clips render natively in the right aspect ratio from the start. fal and WaveSpeed were unaffected (they always inherit the aspect ratio of the input keyframe image).

🆕 Fixed: Hindi / Hinglish voiceover pronunciation

ElevenLabs used to mispronounce romanized Hindi in the voiceover — e.g. a line written mai (मैं) was read like the English word "main," with a hard final consonant instead of the soft nasal sound. The cause: TTS engines apply English letter-to-sound rules to Latin-spelled text, so romanized Hindi gets read as if it were English.

The fix is now automatic: the script writer spells Hindi/Hinglish words in the voiceover line using Devanagari script (मैं, है, नहीं, क्या...) while English words, numbers, and brand names stay in Latin script — natural code-switching, same as how bilingual text is actually written. ElevenLabs reads Devanagari correctly since it's real Hindi text, not a Latin lookalike. Your on-screen captions are untouched — they stay in the usual romanized Hinglish; only the spoken narration changed.

💡 There's also a small backstop for words with no real Devanagari spelling — like a coined brand name — that get respelled directly for ElevenLabs (e.g. a brand name read with an extra syllable gets shortened to the correct 2-syllable spelling). If you catch another word still coming out wrong after listening to a render, flag the exact word and how it should sound — it's a one-line fix to add.

🗣️ UGC mode — creator + product together

UGC mode generates an authentic user-generated-content style ad: a creator on camera holds, uses, and reviews your product — like a real recommendation video. You upload two images (a creator and a product); the app reads them, writes a first-person script that matches your actual creator, and builds each scene's keyframe still with the creator holding/using the product — which you review before any video is made.

When to use this

Use UGC for product ads that need to feel personal and authentic — influencer-style reviews, "honest opinion" demos, unboxings. It reads like a real person recommending the product, not a polished brand ad.

Needs: A creator image + a product image · Anthropic key · a configured video model + image provider (see Models & Cost) · ElevenLabs (optional).
  1. In 🤖 AI Reels, pick the 🗣️ UGC reel type.
  2. Upload a 🧑 Creator Image (the person who'll appear on camera) and a 🛍️ Product Image — both are required.
  3. Write your prompt (e.g. "honest review of my new everyday tote bag") and pick a Script framework — Hook·Value·CTA or PAS work great for UGC.
  4. Set duration, music, and toggles as usual, then Generate.
  5. The script is written as a first-person creator monologue; review & approve, then download/publish.
💡 Use a clear, well-lit creator image (face visible) and a clean product image on a plain background — the cleaner the references, the more faithfully Veo keeps the creator and product looking consistent across scenes.

🎥 Cinematic — one photo → a film-style sequence

Give it one reference image of a character and a storyline. A cinematographer-style director shot-lists a short 15–30s sequence (shot type, lens, depth of field, camera move, cuts), the app generates its own keyframe stills for every shot, you review them, and only then does it spend on video. Built for a strong first draft plus cheap, targeted reshoots — not a flawless one-click final.

When to use this

Use Cinematic when you want a consistent character moving through a directed, film-look sequence — a brand story, a mood piece, a product-in-a-world reel — from a single photo, without filming or hand-picking keyframes. The other AI Reel types generate each clip more independently; Cinematic plans the whole shoot like a DP and locks the character across shots.

Needs: One character reference image · fal.ai API key (for the keyframe stills — Nano Banana) · a configured video model (Google Veo, fal/Kling, or WaveSpeed — see Models & Cost) · Anthropic key · ElevenLabs (optional, voiceover). Set keys in Settings → API Keys. Best at shorter durations.

How it works — the pipeline

StageWhat happens
1. Shot planClaude directs a 4-beat arc (setup → build → turn → payoff) as a per-shot contract: shot type, lens, depth of field, camera move, transition, on-screen caption, and voiceover — plus one global style lock applied to every shot.
2. 🛑 Approval gateYou review the shot list (and edit captions/VO) before any spend.
3. Keyframe stillsThe app generates a still for every shot with Nano Banana, keeping your character consistent (see Character lock below). Cheap.
4. 🛑 Continuity gateClaude-vision checks each still against your reference for face/wardrobe drift. You review thumbnails and reshoot any still right there — still cheap, before video.
5. ClipsEach approved still becomes a short silent clip via your chosen video model. Smart clip length — each clip is generated only as long as its shot needs (snapped to the model's options, e.g. Veo 4/6/8s), not always the 8s max, so short shots cost less.
6. Voice + editPer-shot ElevenLabs voiceover; clip lengths are matched to the voiceover so the whole narration plays. A shot only becomes multiple clips if its narration is longer than one clip.
7. Grade + composeA single colour-match grade across all clips, a soft ~0.3s dissolve between shots (no harsh cuts, voiceover stays in sync), captions burned in, VO + music mixed.
8. DeliverA graded 9:16 MP4, plus an estimated cost and a Review inputs button (see below).

Two approval gates

Cinematic pauses twice — first on the shot plan, then on the generated stills (the Continuity Review tab). The continuity gate is your money-saver: catch a wrong face or outfit on a $0.04 still and reshoot it, instead of paying for a bad video clip. See Review & Approve.

Add a product (optional)

Tick "Include a product" under the character upload to reveal a 🛍️ Product Image upload. When set, the director reads the product and weaves it into the storyline — showing it in the payoff and 1–2 hero shots — and those keyframe stills are generated with your character holding/using the exact product (character-locked as always). Non-product shots stay pure scene-setting. Reshooting a product hero still keeps the product. Leave it off for a character-only cinematic.

How to make a Cinematic reel

  1. Open 🤖 AI Reels and pick the 🎥 Cinematic type.
  2. Upload one clear, well-lit character reference image (face visible). Optional: tick Include a product and upload a clean product image.
  3. Write your storyline and set the Duration.
  4. Click Generate. Review the shot plan (and the estimated cost) → Approve.
  5. Wait for the stills, then on the 🎞 Continuity Review tab check each one; reshoot any that drifted (edit its prompt first if you like) → Approve stills.
  6. The clips, voiceover, grade and compose run automatically → preview, download, or grab it later from History.

✅ Review & Approve

Every mode pauses so you can review before spending. AI reel types pause twice — once on the script (with a cost estimate) and once on the generated keyframe stills.

Why this step exists

Rendering and AI clip generation cost time and API credits. The approval gate lets you catch a bad storyboard before spending them — and tweak the words first.

What you can do in the review modal

TabWhat it shows
Scenes & ScriptOne card per scene/shot. Edit the on-screen text and voiceover line for each before approving. In Cinematic, each card also shows the cinematography (shot type, lens, DOF, camera move, cut).
SummaryThe reel's theme, duration, scene count, and music at a glance.
⚠ Policy ReviewAny content-policy warnings detected in the visuals (e.g. risky words) so you can fix them first.
🎞 Continuity / Keyframe ReviewA thumbnail of every generated still (with a drift verdict for person modes). Edit a still's prompt and Reshoot it in place — no full rerun. Shown for all AI reel types now, not just Cinematic.
💰 Estimated costA banner on the script gate shows the estimated spend (stills + voiceover + clips, priced by your video provider). Approving the script approves the cost.

Every AI reel now pauses twice

All AI reel types (Story / Character / No-Characters / Product / UGC / Cinematic) have two gates:

  1. Gate ① — Script & Cost: review/edit the script (on-screen text + voiceover per scene) and see the estimated cost. Approving approves the spend.
  2. Gate ② — Keyframe review: the app generates a cheap still per scene; preview each, edit its prompt and hit ↻ Reshoot to redo just that one — all before any video is generated.
  3. Only after ② does it spend on video. So a wrong face, outfit or product costs cents to fix, not a wasted clip.

For person modes (Character / UGC / Cinematic) each still also gets a continuity verdict (consistent / minor / drift); minor-drift is advisory, only clear drift is flagged.

💡 The modal is scrollable with a pinned Approve bar — even a 12-still continuity list stays easy to review and approve without losing the button.
ℹ️ You steer the words at Gate ① (on-screen text + voiceover) and the visuals at Gate ② (edit a still's prompt and reshoot). The scene's underlying visual description isn't edited directly — reshooting the still is how you fix a visual.

How cost works — and how to cut it

The one thing to know

Video is billed per second, and each clip is generated at a ~5-second floor — even if your shot only keeps ~2s after trimming. So the two big levers are the model you pick and the number of clips (shots).

LeverEffect
🆕 Smart clip lengthAutomatic — each clip is now generated only as long as its scene needs (Veo 4/6/8s, Kling 5/10s), not always the 8s max. A 3s scene → a 4s Veo clip ≈ half that clip's cost.
🆕 Split-only-when-neededAutomatic — a scene becomes 1 clip unless its narration is longer than one clip, so you're not paying for extra clips per scene.
Cheaper modelKling 2.1 Standard (~$0.045/s) or WAN (~$0.03/s) instead of Kling Pro (~$0.098/s) ≈ 2–3× cheaper.
Longer shotsRaise Shot length so fewer, longer clips cover the reel (fewer floor billings).
Continuity gateReshoot bad stills ($0.04) instead of bad clips ($0.25–0.60).
💡 Iterate on fal → Kling 2.1 Standard (or WAN) to keep drafts cheap, then switch to Kling Pro for the final render once you're happy with the shot list. The cost estimate at the script gate shows the number before you approve.

📤 Publish & Schedule

Push a finished reel straight to your social accounts through Publer, post now or schedule for later — without leaving the app.

Needs: Publer API key + Workspace ID, enabled in Settings → Publer. Click Fetch Accounts there once to load your connected social accounts.

Publish a finished reel

  1. Generate and approve a reel so it finishes rendering.
  2. In the result view, choose Push to Publer.
  3. Select the target social account(s), write your caption.
  4. Post now, or set a schedule time to queue it for later.

The Queue (batch ideas)

In Settings → Queue you can add reel ideas in advance and run them on demand. With auto-run enabled (and an interval), queued ideas generate automatically — and if an idea has Publer push configured, the finished reel is scheduled for you.

💡 Set your default Publer accounts, auto-schedule, and post time once in Settings → Publer so every push pre-fills them.

📁 Download & Output

Every finished reel can be previewed in-app and downloaded as an MP4.

Download a reel

  1. When a job completes, the result view shows a video preview and, for AI reels, an estimated cost.
  2. Click Download to save the .mp4 to your computer.
  3. For Cinematic reels, 📋 Review inputs & cost opens the per-stage cost breakdown and the exact prompts sent to each model — see Models & Cost.

Auto-save to a local folder

In Settings → Output you can set a Local output folder. When set, every finished reel is also copied there automatically — so all your renders collect in one place without manual downloads.

ℹ️ On the hosted server, the output folder lives on the server (e.g. /opt/koofrreels/outputs). Use the in-app Download button to pull a reel to your own machine.
